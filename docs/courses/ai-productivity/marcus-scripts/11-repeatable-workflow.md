# Lesson 11 — Build Your Repeatable Workflow
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Using Copilot once is helpful. Using it the same way every single time is where the real savings come from. And that takes a workflow: a written set of steps for a task, simple enough that anyone on your team could follow it.

The key is to map the workflow before you add any automation. Ask yourself: What starts it? Maybe a new inquiry arrives. Where does the information come from? What's allowed to go into AI? What does the AI do, maybe draft a reply? What does a person check? Who approves? How does it get sent? And where is the result recorded?

Write each step as one line. That's it. That's your SOP, your standard operating procedure.

Let me walk you through one for following up on new leads.

Step one, the trigger: a new inquiry comes in from your website or your phone.
Step two, check: did this person agree to be contacted? If not, stop.
Step three, sort it using your triage matrix from Lesson 5.
Step four, draft: use your prompt card from Lesson 3 to have Copilot write a reply. No private details in the prompt.
Step five, verify the facts, the prices and the promises against your approved information.
Step six, approve and send from your normal business email or phone.
Step seven, record: write down how long it took, whether you had to edit the draft a lot, and what happened.
And always, a stop rule: if anything is unclear or sensitive, it goes to the owner instead.

Do you see how every lesson so far shows up in there? That's on purpose.

Now, don't roll it out to your whole business on day one. Run a small pilot: ten cases, maybe twenty. Then compare against the baseline you recorded back in Lesson 1. Minutes per task. Mistakes. How many needed to be escalated. How customers responded.

And decide what "working" means before you start. For example: "Replies take under three minutes instead of eight, with no more mistakes than before." If the pilot hits that goal, keep it and train your team. If it doesn't, fix the steps, or stop. Remember, more text isn't success. Measured improvement is.

Your assignment: write a step-by-step workflow for the pilot task you picked in Lesson 1, using the lead follow-up as a model. Then create a scorecard with your baseline minutes per task, your pilot minutes per task, how many drafts were approved without major edits, mistakes, and escalations. Run your workflow on at least five made-up or approved real cases, and fill in the scorecard.
