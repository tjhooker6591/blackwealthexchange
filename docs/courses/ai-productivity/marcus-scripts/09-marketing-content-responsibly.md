# Lesson 9 — Marketing Content, Responsibly
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Marketing is where AI saves the most time, and where it causes the most embarrassment. Copilot can write ten captions in the time it takes you to write one. It can also invent a sale you're not running, a date you never set, or a quote from a customer who doesn't exist.

So let's do it the right way.

Start with what's true and valuable about your business. What do you sell? Who is it for? What makes you different? And what are the exact details of any offer you're actually running? Then describe your brand voice in a sentence. Something like: "Friendly, proud of our neighborhood, never pushy."

Give Copilot those facts and that voice, and ask for a version for each channel. A website description is a few clear sentences. A social caption is short, with one call to action. An email needs a subject line and one good reason to open it. When you ask for all three at once, from the same facts, your message stays consistent everywhere.

Before anything gets posted, check every claim, date, price and discount. Don't use a customer's name, photo or review without their permission. And skip the fake urgency. "Only two spots left!" is fine when it's true. When it isn't, you're spending trust you'll need later. Honest marketing is what keeps customers coming back.

Let's practice with a made-up business: an independent, Black-owned bookshop hosting an author reading on Saturday. You haven't confirmed the time, the address on the flyer, or the author yet.

Run the prompt on the lesson page. It sets the voice, warm, proud, community-focused, and asks for a three-sentence website description, two Instagram captions under forty words, and a short email with a subject line. It says to write CONFIRM for the time, the address and the author, and to add no discounts, prizes or customer quotes.

Now check the drafts. The time, address and author should each say CONFIRM. If Copilot slipped in "first ten guests get twenty percent off," take it out. Nobody approved that.

Pick your best caption and write down its call to action, something like "RSVP at the link in our bio." Then make a short claim check: each claim, and whether it's verified or needs confirming.

This is also a great way to show up more often. Most small businesses don't post because they don't have time to write. With your facts and your voice saved, a week of posts can take fifteen minutes.

Your assignment: choose a real or made-up product, service or event. Write down the true facts and your brand voice in a few lines. Have Copilot create a website description, two social captions and one email from them. Check every claim, mark it verified or not, and note your call to action.
