# Lesson 5 — Customer Support and Escalation
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Every business gets every kind of message. "What time do you close?" "Can I move my appointment?" "I was charged twice." And once in a while, something serious. If you treat all of them the same, you waste time on the easy ones and you risk mishandling the hard ones.

So in this lesson we're going to build a triage matrix. Don't let the name scare you. It's a simple table.

Each row is a type of request. The columns are: how serious it is, what the first response should be, what information you need, who handles it, and when it counts as done.

Once you have that table, Copilot can help you sort new messages into the right row, and draft first replies for the easy ones.

Here's how I think about the rows. Low-risk, common questions, like hours, directions, what's included and how to book, are great for AI-drafted replies that you approve. Account changes, billing problems and refunds go to a trained person. And anything involving safety, threats, harassment, or legal or medical issues goes straight to the owner, or the right authority. No waiting on a draft.

One more rule. When you ask Copilot to sort a message, ask it for its reason, and how confident it is. If it's not confident, the message goes to a person. An AI sorter should never make up a policy to fit a message it doesn't understand.

Let's try it with a hair salon.

Build a matrix with four rows. General question, low risk. Booking change, low risk. Billing or refund, medium, the owner handles it. Urgent or safety, high, the owner handles it right away.

Now write five made-up messages. "What time do you close Saturday?" "Can I move my two o'clock to four?" "I was charged twice." "Do you do kids' cuts?" And: "Your stylist burned my scalp."

Paste the matrix and the messages into Copilot, using the prompt on the lesson page. It asks for the row, a one-sentence reason and a confidence level for each message, and tells Copilot not to draft replies for billing, refund or urgent messages.

Now check the results. The double charge should go to the owner. The burn complaint should be marked urgent. If Copilot drafted a reply to the burn complaint offering money, that's a failure, and it means your matrix needs clearer wording. Adjust it until all five land in the right place.

What you've built is a way for anyone on your team, or you on a busy day, to handle the inbox quickly and safely.

Your assignment: build a triage matrix for your own business, with at least four request types. Write five realistic made-up messages, including at least one billing issue and one urgent one. Have Copilot sort them with reasons and confidence, and score how many it got right. If more than one is wrong, improve your descriptions and try again.
