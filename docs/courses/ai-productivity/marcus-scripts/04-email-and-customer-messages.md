# Lesson 4 — Email, Writing and Customer Messages
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

For most small businesses, email is where AI saves time first. You can turn three rough notes into a clear, kind message in a few seconds. But there's one rule that keeps you safe, and I want you to say it with me: Copilot writes the draft. A person sends the message.

Here's why that matters. Copilot is eager to help. And to an AI, "helpful" can mean offering things you never agreed to: a discount, a delivery date, a refund. So you only give it facts you've approved, and you tell it what not to promise.

If a shipment is late and you don't know the new date, say so in your prompt, and tell it not to promise one. If you haven't decided on a refund, tell it not to mention money.

Some messages always need careful human review. Refunds and credits. Complaints that could turn into disputes. Anything legal, health or safety related. And messages to customers who are already upset. For those, use Copilot to organize your thoughts and fix the tone. Then read every sentence before it goes out.

Let me show you how quick this can be.

Say you run a renovation company, and you jot down a note like this: "Johnson kitchen job. Tile delayed. Supplier says next week, maybe. Sorry. Will call Monday."

Paste the prompt from the lesson page. It asks Copilot to turn those notes into a short, warm email with a subject line, to promise no date and no discount, and to end with the next step: we'll call Monday.

Now check every fact in the draft against your note. If Copilot wrote "your tile will arrive Wednesday," delete it. You never said that. If it offered ten percent off, delete that too.

Then add an approval line at the bottom, just for you: "Checked: dates, prices, promises. Approved by," and your initials. It seems small, but it builds the habit.

Finally, save the finished version as a template, with blanks for the customer's name, the job, and the next step. The next time something's delayed, you're done in a minute.

That's the real win here: templates. Most businesses send the same handful of messages over and over. Quote follow-ups. Appointment reminders. Delay notices. Thank-you notes. Build each one once, carefully, with an approval line, and reuse it.

Your assignment is to create three templates with Copilot: a sales or quote follow-up, a delay or problem notice, and a thank-you or meeting recap. Use made-up details. For each one, list every factual claim in it and confirm it's something you'd actually stand behind. Then add your approval line.

When you're done, take the check below. In Lesson 5, we'll go from writing single emails to handling a whole inbox of customer requests, and deciding which ones a person must always handle.
