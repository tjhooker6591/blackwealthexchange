# Lesson 6 — Meetings and Action Items
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Have you ever walked out of a meeting and realized everyone remembers it differently? Who was supposed to call the vendor? Did we decide on the price, or just talk about it? A good written summary fixes that. The problem is, nobody has time to write one. That's where Copilot helps.

You can give Copilot your rough notes, or, if your tools and your team allow it, a meeting transcript, and get a clean summary in under a minute.

I want you to ask for the same four sections every time. One: a short summary of what was discussed. Two: decisions that were actually made. Three: action items, each with an owner and a due date. Four: open questions.

Keeping discussion separate from decisions is important. "We talked about raising prices" is not the same as "We decided to raise prices on June first." Your summary should make that difference obvious.

Now here's the biggest risk. If nobody volunteered to design the flyer, Copilot might still assign it to someone who sounds likely. So tell it plainly: if an action has no owner in the notes, write UNASSIGNED. If it has no date, write NO DATE. Then you fill those gaps with your team, instead of discovering them a week later.

And one more thing before we practice. Always get everyone's permission before you record a meeting or upload a transcript, and follow your organization's rules about where recordings are kept. Some Microsoft 365 Copilot plans can summarize Teams meetings directly. If yours doesn't, typed notes work just fine.

Let's try it. Here are some made-up notes from a launch meeting: "Talked about the Friday launch. Maya will check stock by Thursday. Need a poster, nobody picked. Price stays twenty-five dollars. Jordan asked about delivery partners, no answer yet."

Run the prompt from the lesson page. Then check the result. Maya owns the stock check, due Thursday. The poster should say UNASSIGNED. The twenty-five dollar price is a decision. Delivery partners is an open question.

If Copilot gave the poster to Jordan, or added a date that wasn't in the notes, fix it, and make that instruction even clearer in your template.

Your assignment: write a meeting summary template using the four sections. Run it on made-up notes, or notes from a real meeting that isn't sensitive, with everyone's permission. Check every name, date and decision against the original. Then turn the action items into a register: a simple table of owner, task, due date and status. That register is how things actually get done.
