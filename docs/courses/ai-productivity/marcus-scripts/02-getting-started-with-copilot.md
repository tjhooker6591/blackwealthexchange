# Lesson 2 — Getting Started with Microsoft Copilot
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Welcome back. In this lesson we're going to open Copilot and figure out what you actually have, because that's where a lot of people get confused.

Here's the thing to know: "Copilot" isn't one product. It's a family of Microsoft products that share a name.

There's the free Copilot app and website that anyone with a Microsoft account can use. There's Copilot Chat for people who sign in with a work or school account. And there's Microsoft 365 Copilot, a paid add-on that works inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, and can work with your organization's files and email.

What you see depends on which of those you have, what your license includes, and what settings were chosen by whoever manages your Microsoft account. So two people can both open Copilot and see different buttons. That's normal. Never assume a feature exists because you saw it in an ad, or a video, including this one. Check your own screen.

Next, sign in the right way. Use the account your business approves. If you're on a work account, look for a shield icon or a note saying your data is protected. That means your chats are handled under your organization's agreement with Microsoft. A personal account follows Microsoft's consumer terms instead. Know which one you're using before you paste anything into it.

And for now, practice with made-up information. A made-up customer. A made-up to-do list. Once you know how Copilot behaves, and once we've covered privacy in Lesson 10, you can start using real, non-sensitive material.

Let's run your first test.

Open Copilot from your approved account. Before typing anything, look around and write down what you see. Is there a work-account shield? Can you upload a file? If you open Word or Outlook, is there a Copilot button there too? That list is your feature checklist.

Now paste in the sample prompt from the lesson page. It describes a small cleaning company with nine things on the to-do list this week: reply to quote requests, fix the phone number on the website, order supplies, chase an unpaid invoice, post on Instagram, train a new hire, renew insurance, ask happy clients for reviews, and update the price sheet. It asks Copilot to pick the three most important and explain why, and to say if it needs information it doesn't have.

Before you read the answer, pick your own top three. Then compare.

You'll probably find Copilot makes sensible choices. Following up on money owed and replying to new quote requests usually land near the top. But notice what it can't know. It doesn't know your insurance expires Friday. It doesn't know a big client is about to leave. That's your knowledge, and it's why you stay in charge.

Write down one thing Copilot did well, and one thing it couldn't have known.

Your assignment: sign in with your approved account, fill in your feature checklist, which app, work or personal account, whether file upload works, and whether Copilot shows up inside Word, Excel, Outlook or Teams for you. Then run the to-do list prompt with your own made-up or non-sensitive list, and compare its top three with yours.
