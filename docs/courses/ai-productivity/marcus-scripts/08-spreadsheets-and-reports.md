# Lesson 8 — Spreadsheets and Business Reports
Presenter: Marcus · Target length: about 3–4 minutes · Paste everything below the line into HeyGen.

---

Your numbers tell you how your business is really doing. But a lot of owners avoid spreadsheets, and I understand why. This lesson is about using Copilot to make numbers easier, without letting it fool you.

Copilot can suggest a formula, explain what a column means, recommend a chart, and write a plain-English summary of what happened this week. If you have Microsoft 365 Copilot in Excel, it can work on your sheet directly. Otherwise, paste a small table into the chat.

Here's the catch. AI can misread numbers. It can confuse units, mix up columns, or call something an improvement when it isn't. So before you ask it anything, define your measures in plain words.

Take a booking rate. A booking rate is bookings divided by inquiries. Write that down. Then, when Copilot gives you an answer, check that it used your definition.

Always check totals yourself before you share a report, with a calculator or a simple SUM in your spreadsheet. And watch out for small numbers. Going from two sales to four is technically "one hundred percent growth." It might also just be a lucky week. Ask Copilot to show its calculation, and to tell you when the sample is too small to draw a conclusion.

Let's try it with made-up data. Week one: forty inquiries and eight bookings. Week two: fifty inquiries and ten bookings.

Do the math yourself first. Eight divided by forty is twenty percent. Ten divided by fifty is also twenty percent. So bookings went up, but the booking rate didn't change. More bookings came from more inquiries, not from getting better at converting them.

Now run the prompt from the lesson page. It gives Copilot the data and the definition, asks it to show the calculation for each week, suggest one chart, and write a two-sentence summary without claiming an improvement unless the numbers show one.

Compare its answer with yours. If it says your booking rate "improved," that's exactly the kind of mistake we're watching for. Correct it with a quick follow-up.

A good weekly report is short. One small table. One chart. Two or three honest sentences about what happened, with the caveats. That beats a ten-page report nobody reads.

Your assignment: build a five-week table with inquiries, bookings and revenue, using made-up or non-sensitive numbers. Write down the definition of each measure before you ask Copilot anything. Have it calculate the rates, suggest a chart, and write a short summary. Then check at least three of its calculations yourself, and note any mistakes.
